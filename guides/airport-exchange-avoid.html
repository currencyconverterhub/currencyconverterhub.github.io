Why You Should Never Exchange Money at the Airport

Why airport rates are so bad

Airport exchange bureaus charge the worst rates because they can. Their customers are captive: travellers who have just landed, need local cash, and have no alternative in the terminal. The bureau pays high rent for the location and passes the cost to customers through a wide spread, often ten percent or more, plus a fixed commission. A traveller exchanging 500 dollars can lose 50 dollars or more in a single transaction without realising it, because the loss is hidden in the rate.

The bureaus also rely on the fact that few travellers check the mid-market rate. A board showing a rate that looks close to the real one may actually be the rate for selling currency back, not buying it, and the rate that applies to you is much worse. Even bureaus that advertise no commission make their money from the spread, and no commission does not mean no cost.

What to do instead

The simplest alternative is to use an ATM after you pass through arrivals. Airport ATMs are usually operated by banks and offer rates close to the mid-market, with a total cost of one to three percent depending on your card. Withdraw enough for a day or two of small expenses, and use a fee-free card for everything else. If your card charges a foreign ATM fee, the cost is still usually far below a bureau's spread.

Another option is to arrive with a small amount of local currency obtained cheaply at home, through a multi-currency account or a specialist service, so you can pay for the taxi or bus without touching a bureau. For most destinations you need very little cash on arrival, since cards are accepted at airports, hotels, and taxis in most countries. Check before you travel how cash-dependent your destination is, and plan accordingly.

If you have no choice

Sometimes a bureau is genuinely the only option, for example in a country where ATMs are scarce or your cards do not work. In that case, exchange only the minimum you need for the first day, and find a bank or ATM in town for the rest. Compare the two or three bureaus in the terminal if there is more than one, and always calculate the total you will receive before handing over money. Never exchange a large sum at an airport, and never let urgency push you into a bad rate when a short wait would save real money.

Putting It Into Practice

On your next trip, arrive with a plan for cash: a fee-free card, a small amount of local currency if needed, and the location of a bank ATM after arrivals. When you walk past the bureau in the terminal, check its board against the mid-market rate on your phone and calculate what a 500 unit exchange would actually cost. The gap is usually startling, and seeing it once is enough to break the habit of exchanging at the airport for good. Tell a friend about the difference before their next trip, since most travellers only learn about airport rates after losing money to them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do airport exchange bureaus charge?

Often 10 percent or more in total, through a wide spread plus commission, far more than an ATM or a fee-free card.

Is it better to use an airport ATM?

Usually yes. Bank ATMs at airports typically cost one to three percent in total, far less than a bureau.

How much local cash do I need on arrival?

Usually only enough for a taxi and a day of small expenses. Cards cover most arrival costs in most countries.
