Sending Money Abroad: A Complete Guide to International Transfers

The options compared

There are three main ways to send money abroad: your bank, an online specialist transfer service, and a money transfer app. Banks are familiar and feel safe, but they are usually the most expensive, with total costs of three to five percent and delivery times of two to five working days. Specialist services are typically cheapest, under one percent for major currency pairs, and deliver within hours or a day. Apps vary: some are cheap and fast, others charge high spreads hidden in the rate.

The right choice depends on the amount and the destination. For small amounts to a common currency, the difference between providers may be a few dollars, and convenience matters more. For large amounts, such as a house deposit or tuition fees, the difference can be hundreds or thousands of dollars, and comparing providers is essential. For exotic currencies or countries with strict controls, fewer providers operate, and a bank may be the only reliable option.

What affects cost and speed

Cost depends on the spread, the fee, and the payment method. Paying by bank transfer is usually cheapest, while paying by card is faster but can add a fee. Speed depends on the currencies, the countries, and the time of day: transfers between major currencies during business hours are fastest, while transfers involving weekends, holidays, or additional checks take longer. Some providers offer instant delivery to certain countries and slower delivery to others.

Regulation also affects transfers. Large transfers may trigger identity or source-of-funds checks, which are normal and protect against fraud and money laundering. Providing documents promptly keeps the transfer moving, while missing information causes delays. Some countries limit how much money can be received, or require the recipient to show identification, so check the rules for your destination before sending.

Staying safe

Use a regulated provider with a clear fee schedule and a published exchange rate. Be wary of anyone offering a rate better than the mid-market rate, since that is impossible for a retail customer and a common sign of fraud. Never send money to a stranger based on an email or message, and double-check the recipient's details, because a wrong account number can be difficult or impossible to reverse.

Keep the transfer reference and receipt, and track the delivery through the provider's app. If a transfer is delayed, contact the provider with the reference number rather than sending the money again. For regular transfers, such as monthly support for family, set up a recurring transfer with a trusted provider and review the rate and fees every few months, since the market changes and loyalty does not always pay.

Putting It Into Practice

Before your next international transfer, compare three providers on the total the recipient will receive, and note the delivery time for each. Check whether paying by bank transfer is cheaper than paying by card, and whether the recipient's country has any limits or identification requirements. Keep the reference number and track the delivery, and review your provider choice every six months, since the cheapest option changes as the market evolves. Regular comparison is the only way to keep transfer costs low.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest way to send money abroad?

Usually an online specialist transfer service, under one percent for major currencies, compared with three to five percent at a bank.

How long does an international transfer take?

From a few hours to five working days, depending on the currencies, countries, and checks involved.

Is it safe to send money with an online service?

Yes, if the provider is regulated and shows its rate and fees clearly. Avoid any service offering a rate better than the mid-market rate.
