USD to EUR: Converting Dollars to Euros in 2026

The dollar-euro rate

The US dollar to euro rate is the most traded currency pair in the world, and it moves with the difference between US and eurozone interest rates, inflation, and growth. When the US Federal Reserve raises rates faster than the European Central Bank, the dollar usually strengthens against the euro, and when the ECB catches up, the euro recovers. In recent years the pair has traded roughly between 0.85 and 1.10 euros per dollar, with parity, one dollar to one euro, acting as a psychological level that attracts attention whenever it approaches.

For travellers and senders, the exact rate matters less than the cost of converting. A one percent difference in the rate on a 1,000 dollar conversion is ten dollars, which is real money but smaller than the spread many banks charge. Check the live mid-market rate on our converter before any conversion, and use it as the benchmark for comparing providers.

Cheapest ways to convert USD to EUR

For spending in Europe, a fee-free debit or credit card that charges no foreign transaction fee is usually cheapest, since card networks convert close to the mid-market rate. For sending money to a euro account, an online specialist transfer service typically beats a bank by two to four percent, which on 5,000 dollars saves 100 to 200 dollars. Multi-currency accounts let you hold euros and convert when the rate is favourable, which suits people who travel to Europe regularly.

Cash conversion is the most expensive route. Exchanging dollars for euros at a US bank before departure, or at a European bureau on arrival, usually costs three to five percent, and airport bureaus cost more. If you need euro cash, withdraw it from an ATM in Europe with a fee-free card, which typically costs one to two percent in total, far less than a bureau.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most expensive mistake is accepting dynamic currency conversion, where a shop or ATM offers to charge you in dollars instead of euros. The rate used is poor, and the convenience is an illusion. The second mistake is converting everything at once before a trip, which locks in a single rate and leaves you carrying cash. Converting as you go, with a card and occasional ATM withdrawals, spreads the rate risk and keeps your money safer.

Finally, do not assume your bank's rate is fair just because the fee is zero. A zero fee with a bad rate is often the most expensive option of all. Compare the total euros you receive, not the fee, and switch providers when a better one appears. The dollar-euro market is competitive, and a few minutes of comparison is worth real money.

Putting It Into Practice

Next time you convert dollars to euros, check the live mid-market rate on our converter first, then get quotes from two providers and compare the total euros received. If you are travelling, use a fee-free card for spending and withdraw a small amount of euro cash from an ATM on arrival, declining any offer to charge you in dollars. Keep the receipts for a week and compare the rate you actually got with the mid-market rate; the gap is the true cost, and knowing it will make you a sharper converter next time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many euros do I get for 100 dollars?

It depends on the live rate; at 0.92 euros per dollar you get about 92 euros at the mid-market rate, less after any spread or fee.

What is the cheapest way to convert USD to EUR?

A fee-free card for spending, or an online specialist service for transfers. Avoid airport bureaus and bank cash exchange.

Should I convert dollars to euros before travelling?

Only a small amount for arrival. Use a fee-free card and ATMs in Europe for the rest.
