Canadian Dollar Guide: USD to CAD and the Loonie Explained

What moves the loonie

The Canadian dollar, nicknamed the loonie after the bird on the one-dollar coin, is a commodity currency: its value rises and falls with the price of oil and other resources Canada exports. When oil prices rise, Canada earns more export revenue and the dollar strengthens; when oil falls, the dollar weakens. The loonie also tracks the US dollar closely, since the United States is Canada's largest trading partner and the two economies are deeply linked.

Interest rates matter too. When the Bank of Canada raises rates faster than the US Federal Reserve, the loonie usually gains, and when it lags, the loonie slips. In recent years the USD to CAD rate has traded roughly between 1.30 and 1.40, meaning one US dollar buys about 1.35 Canadian dollars. For anyone converting between the two, the rate is stable enough that provider costs matter more than daily moves.

Cheapest ways to convert USD to CAD

For transfers, online specialist services are usually cheapest, with total costs under one percent, and they deliver to a Canadian bank account within a day or two. Canadian banks charge two to four percent in total, and US banks are similar, so both are expensive for regular transfers. Multi-currency accounts are a good alternative for people who move money between the two countries often, since they can hold both currencies and convert when the rate suits them.

For spending in Canada, a fee-free card is the best option, converting at close to the mid-market rate. Canada is highly card-friendly, with contactless payment accepted almost everywhere, so you need very little cash. Withdrawing Canadian dollars from an ATM is also reasonable, though your bank may charge a foreign ATM fee, so check before travelling.

Travelling with Canadian dollars

Canada's currency comes in polymer banknotes that are durable and hard to counterfeit, and the one and two dollar denominations are coins, the loonie and the toonie. Prices are quoted before tax, so the till price is higher than the shelf price, and tipping of 15 to 20 percent is expected in restaurants. Keep receipts for larger purchases, since visitors can sometimes claim back tax on goods taken home, depending on the province and the scheme.

For cross-border shoppers and workers who earn in one currency and spend in the other, a multi-currency account or a specialist transfer service is worth setting up once and using repeatedly. The savings on each transfer are small, but over a year of regular movement they add up to a meaningful amount, and the convenience of instant conversion is hard to beat.

Putting It Into Practice

If you live or work across the US-Canada border, set up a multi-currency account that holds both dollars and use it for every cross-border payment, noting the saving versus your bank each time. For travel, rely on a fee-free card, since Canada is highly card-friendly, and keep a small amount of cash for tips and small purchases. Remember that prices are quoted before tax and that tipping of 15 to 20 percent is expected, so budget accordingly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Canadian dollars do I get for 100 US dollars?

At 1.36 CAD per USD, about 136 Canadian dollars at the mid-market rate, less after any spread or fee.

Why is the Canadian dollar called the loonie?

After the loon, the bird pictured on the one-dollar coin. The two-dollar coin is nicknamed the toonie.

What is the cheapest way to convert USD to CAD?

An online specialist service for transfers, or a fee-free card for spending in Canada.
